Simplifying ledger corrections until they were safe to ship

The accounts team corrected loan ledgers, such as deductions and disbursement entries, case by case with no standard flow. The first idea was an automatic 'health check' that flagged broken ledgers. I simplified it into something the system could actually do reliably, and tested its core assumption with data first.

01 · ProblemAd-hoc fixes to money records

Corrections to a loan's ledger change what a borrower owes, so they need to be consistent and auditable. Each correction was being handled differently, and there was no record of who changed what and why.

02 · SimplifyDrop what the system can't know

The early design showed red and green pass/fail flags on every ledger. But the system couldn't reliably tell what the "right" ledger was. Red flags it couldn't justify would have created noise and distrust. Over six versions I cut it down to:

  • Two neutral sections, Deductions and Disbursement, showing what's there.
  • A standard way to request a correction, with a reason.
  • Maker-checker approval, and only one open request per loan at a time.

03 · ValidateCheck the assumption before building on it

The simpler design relied on one assumption: each loan has at most one voucher per charge type, so a correction edits it if it exists or adds it if it doesn't. Before writing the PRD I checked it against the data:

  • Across ~1,100 loans, every one had exactly one voucher per charge type.
  • Payment vouchers were mostly one per loan; the few with two or three were loans disbursed in tranches, which became an explicit case in the spec.

04 · ShipPRD and handoff

The PRD has 11 acceptance criteria covering the edit-or-add rule, tranches, the one-open-request lock, approvals and the audit trail, split into PM and developer tickets.

05 · LearningsLess, but true

  • Don't show a signal you can't stand behind.
  • Turn assumptions into queries. Ten minutes of data saved a wrong build.
  • Edge cases found in data (tranches) belong in the spec, not in a bug later.
