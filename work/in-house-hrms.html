Replacing a paid HRMS with our own, from attendance to payroll

Our employees clocked in, applied for leave and got paid through a third-party HR tool that didn't fit how a branch-heavy lender works. I was the PM on moving the whole employee lifecycle into our own platform, one module at a time, without disrupting a single payroll.

UsersEmployees, branch managers, HR, accounts

01 · ProblemAn off-the-shelf tool in a branch-first company

Most of our people work in branches and in the field, not at desks. The vendor tool kept HR data apart from our lending platform, so things like branch hierarchy, field roles and approvals had to be maintained twice. Payroll lived in spreadsheets on top of it.

02 · PlanReplace it module by module

A big-bang switch on payroll is how people don't get paid. I sequenced the move so each module went live and settled before the next one depended on it:

Employee records & hierarchy
→
Attendance & leave
→
Hiring & e-signed letters
→
Payroll & payouts
→
Exit & full-and-final

For a while both systems ran side by side, and attendance moved over gradually as branches switched to the app.

03 · What shippedThe main pieces

  • Attendance in the field app with face verification, leave and regularisation.
  • Paperless hiring: onboarding, offer approval and digitally signed appointment letters.
  • Payroll in-system: salary calculated from attendance, bank accounts verified by penny-drop, compensation history with approvals by HR and accounts.
  • Payouts with checks: incentives, arrears and bonus with maker-checker and CFO approval.
  • Expenses and exits: employee claims with duplicate checks, and a full-and-final settlement flow.

04 · DecisionsSmall fixes that prevented big problems

  • Salary hold check. An employee put on hold after the salary sheet was generated could still get paid. Instead of a new workflow, I added a live status check right before payout verification, and linked it to backend work already in progress.
  • Tight scope on arrears and bonus. I cut manual entry, payslip preview and an extra approval route from v1, and kept a simple upload with maker-checker. It went live quickly and stayed simple to audit.
  • Launch notes checked against the system. Before announcing rent deposit tracking to accounts, I tested the email's claims against the live system and rewrote the parts that didn't match.

05 · LearningsWhat I learned running an internal 0 → 1

  • Internal users still churn, back to spreadsheets. Each module had to be easier than the workaround.
  • Payroll needs boring guarantees: approvals, audit trails and a check right before money moves.
  • Sequence by dependency, not by excitement. Records and attendance first made payroll almost easy.
