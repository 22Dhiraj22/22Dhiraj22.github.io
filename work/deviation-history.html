Giving the approver the full deviation history in one view

When a loan breaks a credit policy limit, the credit officer raises a deviation and a senior approver decides. On one loan these requests could arrive days apart, inside a long chat-style thread, so approvers lost track of what they had already approved or rejected. I redesigned how that history is shown.

RolePM: problem framing, prototypes, PRD
UsersSenior approvers, credit officers

Company, branch and people details are anonymised. Figures are rounded.

01 · ProblemContext lost between requests

Deviations cover things like loan-to-value, repayment burden, interest rate, location mismatch and bureau score. Each one needs approval at the top. Because they come in one at a time, the approver opening request number three often can't see what happened to requests one and two.

02 · Getting the problem rightUnderstand first, then design

My first rule was to document exactly how deviations work today before touching the design. An early draft invented new fields and screens. I rejected it and restated the goal in one line:

In one look, the approver understands which deviations on this loan were already approved or rejected.

03 · OptionsRegister vs. a history button

A separate Deviation tab (register)A full register per loan with summary cards and notifications. Thorough, but another place to look.
A History button inside the approval viewOpens right where the decision is made, covering all 5 deviation types. Smaller to build and impossible to miss.

One detail mattered a lot: the summary shows the approved value against policy, not the latest requested value. Showing the latest request made it look as if limits had already been agreed.

04 · Specifying itEdge cases before code

The final prototype walks through 8 scenarios for developers, including the same value requested again, a rejection followed by a new request, and several deviations raised together.

05 · LearningsWhat this taught me

  • Put information where the decision happens.
  • One misleading number can undo a good screen. Approved vs requested was the whole point.
  • Saying no to a polished but wrong design saved more time than any shortcut.
