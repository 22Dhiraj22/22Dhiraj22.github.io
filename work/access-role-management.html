Fixing account lockouts and role changes for 1,000+ employees

When a field officer got locked out or changed roles, the fix was a manual database update by the tech team. That meant lost working hours for the employee, interruptions for engineers, and no audit trail. I designed a self-serve admin flow to replace it.

01 · ContextAccess is a business problem

Our platform runs on role-based access: a branch manager, a credit officer and an IT manager each see different screens and can take different actions. In a regulated lender, who can do what matters for both productivity and compliance.

02 · ProblemEvery change was a ticket and a database edit

  • Lockouts after failed logins could only be cleared with a direct database update.
  • Role changes (promotions, transfers) had to be made in two separate systems, the core platform and HRMS, and they often got out of sync.
  • No audit trail of who changed whom, when, and why.

"A locked officer in the field can't log a single visit until someone at HO fixes it."

03 · DiscoveryMapping the real process

I handled these requests myself for a few weeks to understand them first-hand. I logged each one: what was asked, who asked, how long it took, and what broke. Patterns:

  • Most requests were simple and repetitive: unlock, or change role.
  • Role changes failed mostly because one of the two systems was missed.
  • Requesters were usually HR or the employee's manager, not IT.

04 · OptionsWhat we considered

A. Write an internal runbook for engineersCheap, but engineers still get interrupted and there's still no audit trail.
B. Auto-unlock after a timeoutFixes lockouts only, and weakens security.
C. Self-serve admin screen with maker–checker (chosen)HR and IT admins unlock accounts and change roles in one place, roles update in both systems together, and every change is logged.

05 · SolutionOne screen, both systems, full audit

  • Permission-scoped: only HR and IT admin roles can see the screen.
  • Maker–checker for role changes that grant more access.
  • An update succeeds in both systems or neither, so the two can no longer drift apart.

06 · ImpactExpected and early results

  • Time to restore access drops from ~1 day to minutes.
  • Engineers stop making direct database edits for access issues.
  • A complete audit trail ready for compliance reviews.

07 · LearningsWhat I took away

  • Doing the manual work first is the fastest discovery. A few weeks of handling tickets myself gave clearer requirements than any meeting.
  • Internal users are users too. Lost field hours are a real cost even if they never show up on a customer dashboard.
